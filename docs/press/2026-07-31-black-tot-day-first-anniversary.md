## The rum company founded on Black Tot Day turns one

**On 31 July 1970, the Royal Navy poured its last daily rum ration. Fifty-six
years later, a spirits company founded by two Army veterans on that same date
marks its first birthday.**

On 31 July 1970, the pipe of Up Spirits sounded across the Royal Navy for the
last time. Sailors wore black armbands. Some ships held mock funerals for the
daily tot, a ration issued in some form for over three centuries. The date
became known as Black Tot Day.

On 31 July 2025, Royal Signals veterans Dan Freeman and Rhys Williams chose
that date to found Jerry Can Spirits. The nod was deliberate. This Black Tot
Day, the company turns one.

Jerry Can Spirits is a British craft spirits house built without investors and
without shortcuts. Its first expression, Expedition Spiced Rum, launched in
April 2026: a spiced rum on a Caribbean rum base, produced in small, numbered
batches at the company's British partner distillery, with real ingredients and
no artificial flavourings. Five per cent of profits go to military charities.

"Black Tot Day is the day the Navy's rum tradition ended, so it felt like the
right day to start something new with it," said Dan Freeman, co-founder. "We
served, we know what the tot meant, and we wanted to build a rum company that
treats that heritage with respect rather than using it as a costume."

In its first year the company launched Expedition Spiced Rum, joined the Armed
Forces Covenant, and built the Field Manual, a free online reference now
covering more than 340 cocktails and 240 ingredients, including a guide to
Black Tot Day itself and the history of the naval rum ration.

"Year one was about proving we could do this properly," said Rhys Williams,
co-founder. "Year two is about going further. We will mark the anniversary the

came before us."

The company will mark the day with a tot at midday on 31 July, in line with
naval tradition.

### Notes to editors

- Black Tot Day, 31 July 1970: the final day of the Royal Navy's daily rum
  ration. The ration traces to the capture of Jamaica in 1655; Admiral Edward
  Vernon ordered it diluted into grog in 1740; it was reduced through the
  nineteenth century and abolished in 1970 after the Admiralty concluded spirits
  and modern weapons systems did not mix. In compensation, the Treasury paid
  2.7 million pounds into the Sailors' Fund. The Royal New Zealand Navy
  continued the tot until 1990. The order to splice the mainbrace, an extra
  issue of rum, survives and requires royal or Admiralty authority.
- Jerry Can Spirits Ltd was founded on 31 July 2025 by Dan Freeman and Rhys
  Williams, both veterans of the Royal Corps of Signals. The company is
  veteran-owned and bootstrapped. Five per cent of profits support military
  charities.
- Expedition Spiced Rum (70cl, 40% ABV) launched in April 2026 and is sold at
